What is a Wet Belt?

The Ford Transit 2.0 EcoBlue engine uses a wet timing belt — a timing belt that runs inside the engine, submerged in oil. Unlike traditional timing belts that sit outside the engine, this design is enclosed within the engine block.

While Ford designed this for quieter running and reduced maintenance, the wet belt is a known weak point. Over time, the belt can deteriorate, stretch, or snap — and when it does, the consequences are severe.

A failed wet belt can cause catastrophic engine damage, including bent valves, damaged pistons, and in the worst cases, a completely destroyed engine requiring full replacement costing thousands of pounds.

Prevention is simple and far cheaper than the cure. We recommend replacing the wet belt before it becomes a problem.

Warning Signs to Look For

If you notice any of these, don't wait — get your belt checked immediately

🔊

Unusual Engine Noise

Rattling, ticking, or whining from the engine, especially on startup or at idle.

🚨

Engine Warning Light

Check engine light appearing on the dashboard. Don't ignore it.

⚡

Loss of Power

Engine feeling sluggish, hesitating, or losing power under acceleration.

🛢️

Metal in Oil

Metallic particles or debris found in the engine oil during a service.

💨

Rough Running

Engine misfiring, running rough, or vibrating more than usual.

📏

High Mileage

Over 80,000 miles without a belt change? You're on borrowed time. Get it checked.
