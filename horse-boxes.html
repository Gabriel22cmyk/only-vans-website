❓ Question-Based Keywords & Featured Snippets
What is included in a horse box service?

A comprehensive horse box service includes oil and filter changes, brake fluid checks, tire inspection and rotation, lighting system testing, brake pad assessment, suspension inspection, and engine diagnostics to ensure your equestrian vehicle is safe for transport.

How often should I service my horse box?

Horse boxes should be serviced annually or every 10,000 miles, whichever comes first. Regular maintenance prevents mechanical failures, ensures passenger and animal safety, and maintains MOT compliance throughout the year.

What are the common horse box MOT failures?

Common MOT failures include faulty brakes, worn tires, broken lights, steering problems, suspension issues, engine warning lights, and structural rust. Regular maintenance and pre-MOT inspections can prevent most failures.

How much does a horse box MOT cost?

MOT testing costs approximately £55-75 depending on your location and vehicle type. Additional repair costs vary based on identified faults, but preventative servicing typically reduces MOT failure rates and overall repair expenses.

Can you repair horse box electrical issues?

Yes, we provide comprehensive electrical diagnostics and repair services including lighting systems, charging systems, dashboard warning lights, and towing electrics. Modern diagnostic equipment helps identify and resolve issues quickly.

What should I check before towing my horse box?

Before towing, check tire pressure and tread, brake lights and indicators, brake fluid and pads, steering responsiveness, suspension, mirrors, and ramp safety mechanisms. Our pre-towing inspection service identifies any safety concerns.

Do you repair horse box ramps?

Yes, we repair and maintain horse box ramps including hinges, springs, hydraulic systems, and locking mechanisms. Proper ramp function is essential for safe horse loading and unloading.

How do I know if my horse box needs brake repairs?

Warning signs include brake noises (squealing, grinding), spongy or soft brake pedal, longer stopping distances, dashboard warning lights, or vibration when braking. These indicate potential brake system issues requiring immediate attention.
