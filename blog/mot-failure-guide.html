Top 10 Reasons Vans Fail Their MOT

The most common MOT failure points for commercial vehicles — and how to avoid them.

Nobody wants to hear their van has failed its MOT. It means extra cost, time off the road, and the stress of getting it fixed before you can legally drive again. But most MOT failures are preventable — and many are cheap to fix if you catch them early.

Here are the 10 most common reasons vans fail their MOT, based on what we see every week at Only Vans.

1. Lights and Bulbs

The #1 reason for MOT failure. A blown headlight, brake light, indicator, or number plate light is an instant fail. It's also the easiest and cheapest thing to fix — most bulbs cost under £10.

How to avoid it: Walk around your van before the MOT. Turn on headlights, indicators, brake lights, reverse lights, fog lights, and number plate lights. Replace anything that's out.

2. Tyres

Tyres are the second most common failure point. The legal minimum tread depth is 1.6mm across the central three-quarters of the tyre. Uneven wear, bulges, cuts, and cracks are also failure points.

How to avoid it: Check tread depth with a 20p coin — if the outer band of the coin is visible, the tyre is below legal limit. Check all four tyres plus the spare (if tested).

3. Brakes

Worn brake pads, corroded discs, seized calipers, and low brake fluid are all common failures. Vans carry heavy loads, so brakes wear faster than on cars.

How to avoid it: Listen for squealing or grinding when braking. If the brake pedal feels spongy or the van pulls to one side, get the brakes checked before the MOT.

4. Suspension

Worn shock absorbers, broken springs, damaged bushings, and play in steering components. Commercial vans take a beating from heavy loads and rough roads.

How to avoid it: If your van bounces excessively over bumps, clunks over potholes, or the steering feels loose, the suspension needs attention.

5. Windscreen

Chips and cracks in the driver's line of vision (a 290mm-wide strip in front of the steering wheel) are a fail if larger than 10mm. Outside that zone, the limit is 40mm.

How to avoid it: Get chips repaired early — most can be fixed for £30-50 before they spread into cracks that require a full windscreen replacement.

6. Exhaust and Emissions

Diesel vans are tested for exhaust emissions. A poorly maintained engine, blocked DPF (diesel particulate filter), or faulty EGR valve can cause high emissions and an MOT fail.

How to avoid it: Regular servicing keeps emissions in check. If your van produces excessive smoke (black, blue, or white), get it diagnosed before the MOT. A good motorway run can help regenerate a clogged DPF.

7. Mirrors

Cracked, loose, or missing mirrors are an MOT failure. Van mirrors take a lot of abuse — tight car parks, narrow lanes, loading bays.

How to avoid it: Check all mirrors are secure, adjustable, and free of cracks that obscure your view. Replace damaged mirrors before the test.

8. Wipers and Washers

Worn wiper blades that don't clear the windscreen properly, and an empty washer fluid reservoir, are both MOT failures.

How to avoid it: Replace wiper blades if they streak, skip, or leave smears. Top up washer fluid. Takes 5 minutes and costs under £20.

9. Number Plates

Damaged, faded, incorrectly spaced, or non-standard number plates fail the MOT. Plates must be clearly readable from 20 metres.

How to avoid it: Check your plates are clean, undamaged, and use the correct font and spacing. Replacement plates cost around £15-25 each.

10. Seatbelts

Frayed, damaged, or non-retracting seatbelts are an instant fail. The buckle must lock securely and release cleanly.

How to avoid it: Test all seatbelts — pull them fully out and let them retract. Click the buckle in and out. Check the webbing for cuts or fraying.
